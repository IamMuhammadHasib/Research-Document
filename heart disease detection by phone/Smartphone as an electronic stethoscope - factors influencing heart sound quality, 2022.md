# Paper Summary: Smartphone as an Electronic Stethoscope – Factors Influencing Heart Sound Quality

Journal: European Heart Journal – Digital Health (2022)\
Authors: Hongxing Luo et al.\
Study focus: Evaluating whether smartphones can record good-quality heart sounds when used by non-medical individuals.&#x20;

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9708017/

PMC



### 1. Background

- Traditional stethoscopes require clinical training.
- Smartphones have sensitive built-in microphones.
- They may be used as low-cost electronic stethoscopes.
- Factors affecting recording quality were not well understood.

### 2. Aim of the Study

- Assess smartphone ability to record heart sounds.
- Identify factors affecting recording quality.
- Investigate the effects of:
  - Smartphone model
  - Age
  - Sex
  - Body Mass Index (BMI)

### 3. Methodology

- Used an iPhone application called Echoes.
- Participants recorded their own heart sounds.
- No medical training was required.
- Recordings were visually assessed for quality.
- Heart sounds were classified as:
  - Good quality
  - Unsure quality
  - Bad quality

### 4. Study Population

- 1,148 users participated.
- 7,597 heart sound recordings collected.
- Participants were non-medical individuals.
- Data were collected between May and October 2021.

### 5. Main Results

Recording quality

- 74.6% recordings were good quality.
- 6.2% were unsure quality.
- 19.2% were bad quality.
- 80.1% of users achieved at least one good recording.

Number of attempts

- Approximately 60% succeeded on the first attempt.
- Success increased to around 75% within three attempts.
- Most users learned the technique within three attempts.

Smartphone model

- iPhone versions had no major effect on recording success.
- iPhone 7 showed relatively lower success.

Age

- Older age tended to reduce first-attempt success.
- The difference disappeared after three attempts.

Sex

\- No significant difference between males and females.

BMI

- BMI generally did not significantly affect recording quality.
- Users with BMI above 30 showed lower recording success.

### 6. Important Heart Sounds

- S1: First heart sound (closure of mitral and tricuspid valves).
- S2: Second heart sound (closure of aortic and pulmonary valves).
- S3 and S4: Additional heart sounds that were also visible in some recordings.

### 7. Advantages of Smartphone Stethoscopes

- Low cost.
- Easy to use.
- No additional recording equipment required.
- Allows home-based heart sound recording.
- Recordings can be stored and analyzed digitally.
- Potentially useful in remote areas with limited healthcare facilities.

### 8. Potential Clinical Applications

- Remote cardiac monitoring.
- Screening for valvular heart diseases.
- Detection of abnormal heart sounds and murmurs.
- Monitoring heart rhythm abnormalities.
- Supporting future AI-based heart sound analysis.

### 9. Limitations

- Participants may not represent the general population.
- Heart sound quality was assessed visually.
- Assessment may involve observer bias.
- Clinical diagnostic accuracy was not investigated.
- Further studies are required before clinical application.

### 10. Conclusion

- Smartphones can record good-quality heart sounds without medical training.
- Around 80% of users achieved at least one good-quality recording.
- Most users succeeded within three attempts.
- Age and possibly high BMI may influence recording quality.
- Smartphone-based electronic stethoscopes have potential for affordable remote cardiac monitoring.

Overall key message: Smartphone microphones can potentially function as low-cost electronic stethoscopes, allowing ordinary people to record their heart sounds at home. However, further clinical validation is needed before using them for diagnosis.
