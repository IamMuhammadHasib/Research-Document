# Paper Summary: Smartphone for Heart Sound Measurement in Hospital: Feasibility and Influencing Factors

Journal: European Heart Journal – Digital Health (2025)\
Study focus: Evaluating the feasibility of using smartphones to record heart sounds in real hospital environments.&#x20;

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12088729/

PMC



### 1. Background

- Traditional stethoscopes require clinical expertise.
- Digital stethoscopes can record and store heart sounds.
- Smartphones have built-in microphones that may perform similar functions.
- Hospital environments contain background noise that can affect recording quality.

### 2. Aim of the Study

- Evaluate smartphone-based heart sound recording in hospitals.
- Assess the quality of recorded heart sounds.
- Identify factors affecting recording quality.
- Investigate the effects of environmental noise and patient characteristics.

### 3. Methodology

- Used the FonoCheck Android application.
- Used a Xiaomi Redmi 10A smartphone.
- Recorded heart sounds directly from patients' chests.
- Recording duration: 20–50 seconds.
- Two cardiologists independently assessed recording quality.
- Examined environmental and patient-related factors.

### 4. Study Population

- 296 hospitalized patients.
- Data collected between 19–24 January 2023.
- Patients were recruited from different hospital departments.
- Included emergency rooms, ICUs and general wards.

### 5. Main Results

Recording quality

- 86% (254 patients) had good-quality recordings.
- 14% had recordings of lower quality.
- Two assessors showed over 90% agreement.

Environmental noise

- Breathing sounds were the most common source of noise.
- Talking and conversation affected recording quality.
- Patient movement caused unwanted sounds.
- Medical equipment produced background interference.

Hospital departments

- Emergency room: 67% good-quality recordings.
- Respiratory ICU: 78%.
- General ICU: 82%.
- General wards generally showed better recording quality.

Age

- Older age significantly reduced recording quality.
- Patients above 80 years had approximately 70% good-quality recordings.
- Recording quality was generally better in younger patients.

Sex and BMI

- No significant differences between males and females.
- BMI did not significantly affect recording quality.

Body position

\- Sitting and lying positions showed no significant difference.

Comorbidities

- Heart failure did not significantly affect recording quality.
- Atrial fibrillation had no significant effect.
- Coronary artery disease had no significant effect.
- COPD and diabetes had no significant effect.

### 6. Importance of Digital Filtering

- A 20–150 Hz bandpass filter was applied.
- Filtering helped reduce unwanted background noise.
- Good-quality recordings increased from 78% to 88% during auscultatory assessment.
- Improved identification of S1 and S2.

### 7. Advantages of Smartphone Stethoscopes

- Low-cost alternative to digital stethoscopes.
- Easy bedside heart sound recording.
- Heart sounds can be stored for future analysis.
- Allows repeated monitoring.
- Potential for remote patient monitoring.
- Can support AI-based heart sound analysis.

### 8. Potential Clinical Applications

- Bedside cardiac assessment.
- Remote cardiac monitoring after hospital discharge.
- Screening for abnormal heart sounds.
- Development of AI-based cardiac diagnostic systems.
- Monitoring patients with cardiovascular diseases.

### 9. Limitations

- Only 296 patients were included.
- Only one smartphone model was tested.
- Recordings were collected by a single researcher.
- Visual and auditory assessments may involve subjective bias.
- Did not establish diagnostic accuracy for heart diseases.
- Detection of murmurs and S3/S4 heart sounds was not evaluated.

### 10. Conclusion

- Smartphones can successfully record good-quality heart sounds in hospitals.
- Approximately 86% of recordings were of good quality.
- Environmental noise is an important factor affecting recording quality.
- Older age negatively affects recording quality.
- Sex, BMI, body position and comorbidities showed no significant effects.
- FonoCheck has potential as a low-cost bedside cardiac monitoring tool.

### Overall Key Message

Smartphones can be used as low-cost electronic stethoscopes in real hospital environments, with approximately 86% good-quality recordings. However, background noise and older patient age can reduce recording quality. Further research is needed to establish their diagnostic accuracy.
